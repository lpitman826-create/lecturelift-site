        Five 30-minute gym sessions for busy students

        Short, simple sessions you can fit between lectures. Pick one, then use the planner to find the gap.

        These are general examples, not personalised advice. If you have an injury or a health condition, check with a doctor or a qualified trainer before starting a new routine.

        Before you start
        • Warm up for about 5 minutes with easy cardio and some light reps of the first exercise.
        • Choose weights that leave you 1 or 2 reps in reserve at the end of each set.
        • Rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets so each session stays near 30 minutes.
        • Stop if you feel sharp pain, and ask a trainer to check your technique.
        1. Full-body strength
        • Goblet squat: 3 sets of 8 to 10
        • Dumbbell bench press or push-ups: 3 sets of 8 to 10
        • One-arm dumbbell row: 3 sets of 10 each side
        • Romanian deadlift with light dumbbells: 3 sets of 10
        • Plank: 3 holds of 30 seconds
        2. Upper body push and pull
        • Incline dumbbell press: 3 sets of 8 to 10
        • Lat pulldown or assisted pull-up: 3 sets of 8 to 10
        • Seated shoulder press: 3 sets of 10
        • Cable or dumbbell row: 3 sets of 10
        • Biceps curls and triceps pushdowns: 2 sets of 12 each
        3. Lower body and core
        • Split squat: 3 sets of 8 each leg
        • Hip thrust: 3 sets of 10
        • Hamstring curl or glute bridge: 3 sets of 12
        • Calf raise: 3 sets of 15
        • Dead bug: 3 sets of 10 each side
        4. Conditioning circuit

        Do 5 rounds of the 5 stations below. Work for 40 seconds and rest for 20 seconds at each station, which is about 5 minutes a round.

        • Rower or bike
        • Goblet squat
        • Push-ups
        • Step-ups
        • Plank
        5. Mobility and recovery

        A gentle session for rest days or the day after a hard game or workout.

        • Easy bike or walk: 5 minutes
        • Hip flexor stretch: 2 holds of 30 seconds each side
        • Thoracic spine rotations: 10 each side
        • Hamstring stretch: 2 holds of 30 seconds each leg
        • Band pull-aparts or shoulder circles: 2 sets of 12
        • Slow breathing: 2 minutes

        Fitting it in

        • Use your gapsPut sessions in the free hours the planner finds, instead of waiting for a perfect day.
        • Pack the night beforeA ready bag removes one reason to skip.
        • Go shorter in busy weeksA 30-minute session beats a skipped one, so drop the length when deadlines hit.

        Health and safety

        Last updated 21 November 2026. Training should make you healthier, so please look after yourself.

        Please read this first

        LectureLift gives general fitness information for healthy adults. It is not medical advice and it doesn’t replace a doctor, physio or qualified coach. You use the plans, exercises and tips at your own risk.

        Before you start

        • If you have an injury, a health condition, are pregnant or recovering from illness, or are unsure whether exercise is right for you, speak to your GP or a physio first.
        • If you are new to training, start lighter than you think you need to and build up slowly.

        While you train

        • Warm up before you lift or run, and cool down afterwards.
        • Stop straight away if you feel sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain or unusual breathlessness. Sore muscles are normal; pain in a joint is not.
        • Good technique matters more than heavy weight. Ask gym staff or a qualified coach to check your form, especially on big lifts like squats and deadlifts.
        • Use a spotter or safety bars for heavy pressing and squatting, and make sure equipment is in good condition.
        • Drink water, eat enough, and rest. Sleep and recovery are part of the plan.

        Take extra care with

        • Sport-specific moves such as Nordic hamstring curls, neck holds, jumps and bounds. Start with easy versions and small numbers, and ask a coach if you are unsure.
        • Running: wear bright or reflective clothing in the dark, stay aware of traffic, keep the volume low enough to hear what is around you, and let someone know your route.
        • Heat and cold: adjust your session when it is very hot or icy.

        Remember

        The programmes and exercise descriptions are general guidance and not personalised to you. You are responsible for deciding what is safe for your body. If you are worried about your health, get advice from a professional.

        In an emergency, call 999. For non-emergency health advice in the UK, call NHS 111.

        Frequently asked questions

        Short answers about how LectureLift works and where it’s going.

        Who is LectureLift for?

        University students who want to train regularly but struggle to fit it around lectures, deadlines, sport, and a budget.

        How does the planner work?

        Click the slots where you have lectures or other commitments (club training, a match, work), then pick your goal, training days, preferred time, and session length. LectureLift fits gym sessions and runs into your free gaps and marks everything else as free time.

        What do Smart rest days do?

        When it’s on, LectureLift plans no training on a match day or the day before it, uses an easy session the day after, and avoids more than two gym days in a row. You can switch it off under Rest days in the planner.

        Can I train with a friend?

        Yes. The With a mate page shows the hours you and a friend are both free, using your week from the planner and the slots you mark as busy for them.

        Do I need an account?

        No. The planner, programmes and tips work without signing up. You can also create a profile for your own space, saved on this device. Accounts that follow you between devices are coming later, and the early list gets them first.

        Where is my week saved?

        Only in your own browser, on the device you’re using. Nothing is sent to us, so a different phone or browser starts fresh. To move your plan to another device, make a backup code on the Plus page and paste it into the other one.

        Do I need a gym membership?

        LectureLift plans your sessions around your timetable. It doesn’t include gym access at the moment, so you’d use your university gym or any gym you already have.

        Is it free?

        The planner is free to use. Some extras will be paid, so see the Pricing page, and join the early list to tell us what would feel fair.

        Is there an app?

        Not yet. For now LectureLift is a website. We’re testing the idea with students before building more.

        Are the example plans personalised training advice?

        No. The sessions shown are general examples. If you have an injury or a health condition, check with a doctor or a qualified trainer before starting a new routine.

        Join the early list and tell us your biggest struggle with the gym. We read every response.
